# 6. Sales call script and objection handling

## Before the call (10 minutes)
- [ ] Open the lead in the CRM and read the research notes and personalisation line.
- [ ] Watch their product video (if any) and skim their last 5 LinkedIn posts.
- [ ] Pick 2 portfolio pieces closest to their product (the same kind of UI, AI, fintech or devtools).
- [ ] Have the plans (section 03) and the proposal template (section 07) open.

## Discovery call: 25 minutes
Aim to **understand, then recommend one plan**. Let them talk for 70% of the call.

**1. Open (2 min)**
> "Thanks for making time, {{first_name}}. Here's what I had in mind: I'd like to understand what you're launching and how you handle video today, then I'll show you two examples close to {{company}} and suggest what I'd do. If it's not a fit, I'll tell you. Sound good?"

**2. Situation (5 min)**
- "Walk me through what {{company}} is launching in the next 3 months."
- "How do you handle video today: in-house, freelancers, a studio, or not at all?"
- "Who is responsible for marketing content, and who signs off?"

**3. Problem (6 min)**
- "What happened with your last launch? How was it announced?"
- "What's frustrating about how video gets made now?" (Listen for: slow, re-briefing, quality, cost, nobody owns it.)
- "If nothing changes, what does the next launch look like?"

**4. Impact (4 min)**
- "How do prospects first understand the product today: a demo call, the site, LinkedIn?"
- "If every launch had a proper video and social cut-downs, what would that change for sales or fundraising?"
- "Is there a deadline, such as an event, a launch date or a board meeting?"

**5. Show (4 min)**
Share your screen and play the two pieces you picked (30–60 seconds each).
> "This is close to what I'd do for your [feature]. Same approach: script, then UI motion, then cut-downs."

**6. Recommend and close (4 min)**
> "Based on what you said, [X launches, need for Y], I'd recommend **[Pilot / Starter / Growth]**. That's [deliverables] for [price] + GST. Most teams start with the pilot so you see it on your own product first, and it's fully credited if you continue.
> Shall I send the proposal today? And who else needs to see it?"

**Book the next step before you hang up:** a date to talk about the proposal, in the calendar, right there on the call.

## After the call (within 2 hours)
1. Fill in the proposal template (section 07) and send it.
2. In the CRM: log the call (type "Meeting"), move the lead to **Proposal**, set the deal value. The proposal follow-up sequence starts automatically.

## Objection handling
Use **Acknowledge → Ask → Answer**. Never argue.

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| **"It's too expensive."** | "Fair to ask. What are you comparing it to?" Then: "A single 60-second SaaS explainer from a studio in India usually runs ₹50,000 to ₹1 lakh. Growth gives you an explainer plus 12 short videos and ad variants every month for ₹95,000. If budget's tight, Starter at ₹55,000 or the ₹45,000 pilot gets you started." |
| **"We'll just hire a freelancer."** | "That can work. What's your experience been so far?" (Usually: unreliable.) "The difference is continuity and range: one team that knows your product, covering both motion and editing, with agreed turnaround times. And no hiring or managing." |
| **"We're hiring in-house."** | "Makes sense long-term. How long do you expect hiring to take? We often cover the gap, then stay on for motion and explainer work an in-house editor doesn't do. Month-to-month after 3 months, so there's no lock-in." |
| **"AI tools can do this now."** | "For quick walkthroughs, AI tools are great, and we use AI where it actually helps. What they can't do yet is decide the story: what to show, in what order, for which buyer. That's the part that makes a launch video work." |
| **"We don't have enough content to fill a plan."** | "Most teams think that at first. One feature launch is usually one explainer plus 4–6 cut-downs. Founder clips come from calls you already do. But if it's genuinely low volume, the pilot or Starter is the right size." |
| **"Send me a proposal and I'll think about it."** | "Happy to, it'll be with you today. So it's useful: is the question the budget, the timing, or whether we're the right team? And when should we talk it over? How about Thursday?" |
| **"I need to check with my co-founder/CEO."** | "Of course. What do you think they'll ask? I'll make sure the proposal answers it. Would a 15-minute call with both of you be easier?" |
| **"Can you do a free sample?"** | "We already made you the 15-second teaser. Beyond that, the pilot is our paid trial, and if you're not happy with the first draft we'll redo it once, and if you're still not happy you don't pay the second half." |
| **"3-month minimum is too long."** | "Understood. The first month is mostly learning your product, and months 2–3 are where speed and quality jump, which is why the minimum exists. If you'd rather test first, the pilot has no commitment." |
| **"Quality concerns: can you match our brand?"** | "That's the most important question. Step 1 of every engagement is a brand profile: tone, references and do's and don'ts. You approve the script and style frames before any animation starts." |
| **"Your turnaround is too slow / we need it in 3 days."** | "What's the date driving that? For short-form, 48 hours is standard on Growth. For a motion piece, rush is 5 working days at +30%. Let's see if the script can be locked today." |
| **"We had a bad experience with an agency."** | "Sorry to hear that. What went wrong?" Then answer that specific point with your process: fixed deliverables, review rounds, one point of contact, and turnaround in writing. |

## Closing lines
- **Assumptive:** "Shall I send the invoice for the pilot so we can start the script on Monday?"
- **Choice:** "Would you rather start with the pilot, or go straight to Growth for the launch next month?"
- **Timeline:** "To hit your [date] launch, we'd need the script locked by [date]. Want to make that work?"
