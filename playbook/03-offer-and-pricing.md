# 3. Offer and pricing

## The core offer (one sentence)
> **"Record your episode. Within 48 hours you get a polished YouTube edit plus a week of captioned short clips, ready to post. You never touch editing again."**

**Entry offer: the $500 Pilot.** One episode becomes 1 long-form edit and 6 clips, delivered within 5 days. The full $500 is credited to the first month if they sign within 14 days. Use it for "not right now" or "not sure it works" objections, never as an opening offer.

**Free sample (outreach only):** 1–2 clips cut from their latest public episode. It costs you about 1 editor-hour and is your strongest outreach asset.

### Terms
- Initial 3-month term, then month-to-month with 30 days' notice. A podcast takes about 90 days to show results; a shorter term means clients churn before the work pays off.
- Billed monthly in advance (Stripe subscription or invoice due on receipt).
- **Turnaround guarantee:** if we miss 48 hours on more than 2 deliveries in a month, the client may cancel with no notice and that month is 50% off.
- Payment terms and scope go in a simple agreement. Have a lawyer review your template once, before the first client.

### Founding-client pricing (your first 5 clients only)
Offer **25% off for the first 3 months** (Engine at about $1,500) in exchange for: a written testimonial at day 45, permission to use their metrics in a case study at day 60, and one referral introduction if they're happy. Always call this "founding-client pricing" so it reads as a limited offer, not a discount you'll repeat.

## Why these prices (check your numbers before you change them)

Working assumptions: freelance editor at **$20/hour** (experienced offshore editors cost roughly $15–35/hour), about 5 hours per long-form edit, about 0.75 hours per clip using AI tools for the first rough pass, $25 per thumbnail, and 10% for software, storage and payment fees.

| Plan | Editor hours | Editor cost | Thumbs | Tools/fees (10%) | **Total cost** | **Gross margin** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clips $997 | 24h | $480 | — | $100 | $580 | **42%** |
| Engine $1,997 | 20h + 18h = 38h | $760 | $100 | $200 | $1,060 | **47%** |
| Studio $3,997 | 40h + 30h = 70h | $1,400 | $200 | $400 | $2,000 | **50%** |

- Aim for **at least 50% gross margin**. Margins rise as your editors get faster with each client's style guide. Track actual hours per client for the first month, and reprice anyone under 40%.
- **Clips is your lowest-margin plan.** Use it as the downgrade option to keep a client, not as the plan you lead with.
- While you do the editing yourself, these "costs" are your own wages. That's fine, but price as if you'll hire, or you'll never be able to.

### Revenue math
- **$10k MRR** = 5 Engine clients. Working backwards with the targets in `13-kpis.md`: 5 clients ÷ 30% close rate = 17 proposals; ÷ 70% of calls that get a proposal = 24 calls; ÷ 40% of replies that book a call = 60 positive replies. At a 5% reply rate that is **about 1,200 prospects contacted**; at 8% (realistic once you send free samples) it is about 750. At 100 new prospects a week, that is roughly **2–3 months**. Referrals and inbound content shorten it.
- **$25k MRR** = about 8 Engine + 2 Studio. By then you need 2–3 freelance editors and an account manager (or yourself in that role).

## Upsells and cross-sells (from month 2)
- Clips → Engine: "Your clips are working. A matching YouTube edit and thumbnail brings that audience into the full episode."
- Engine → Studio: when they add a second weekly show or want posting done for them.
- Add-ons: newsletter or blog written from each episode (+$400/month), a podcast trailer or ad cut ($300 one-off), and a guest-clip pack that lets each guest share their own clips (+$200/month).
