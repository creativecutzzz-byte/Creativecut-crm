## 3. Audience

- **Primary:** Indian beginner editors who watch GTA content and want to make their first gaming Short. They may have only a phone (often a mid-range Android), patchy data, and no game or console. English may be their second language.
- **Secondary:** small gaming creators who want to cut Shorts from their own livestreams.

## 4. Voice and style rules

1. Plain English. Short sentences (aim for under 20 words). One idea per paragraph.
2. Talk to the reader as "you". Friendly, direct, no hype.
3. Keep standard editing words in English and define them the first time: trim, timeline, captions, export, aspect ratio, 9:16, CPM, overlay, hook, payoff.
4. Instructions are numbered steps that start with a verb ("Tap", "Drag", "Check").
5. Every mission ends with a **"You now have"** box: a concrete output used in the next mission.
6. Indian context: rupees for prices, UPI, PAN/KYC, mid-range Android first, iPhone second.
7. No slang the reader must decode. No "guaranteed", "easy money", "passive income".

## 5. Product promise (exact)

> Use a phone and free tools to turn a moment from footage you have permission to use into a vertical Short, then prepare it for submission to an eligible clipping campaign.

- The toolkit itself is **paid**. "Free tools" means the editing and planning tools used in the workflow.
- The book teaches a skill and a checklist. It does **not** promise approval, views, payouts, or monetization.

## 6. Claim rules

- Platform rules (payouts, minimums, fees, thresholds, eligibility, tool limits) appear as fact **only** when `sources.md` records a primary source with a date checked. Otherwise the book says "check the current brief/help page" and gives the reader the question to ask.
- All earnings numbers are worked examples, labeled "example", with gross vs net made explicit.
- The practice case is **fictional** and labeled as such on every page that uses it. Real campaigns are named only when they appear in `sources.md`.
- Prices for client work are labeled "illustrative".
- No game footage, logos, or Rockstar artwork are reproduced. All art is original. The Rockstar artwork page was used only as a mood reference for palette (sunset, dusk).

## 7. Reader journey (seven missions, one practice case)

Brief → timestamped transcript → highlight → edit plan → vertical frame → submission review → reward calculation.

The case runs from page 4 ("Spot the Rejection") to Mission 7. Every step can be done from the PDF alone. The reader does not need the game, a console, or any video file to complete the exercises.
